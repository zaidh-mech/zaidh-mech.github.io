Reverse engineering / Industrial training

FLOD Glue-Pellet Hopper

A configurable replacement hopper developed around an existing Flat Line On Demand machine to improve pellet flow, reduce blockage and add a compact motor-driven clearing mechanism.

Context
MAS Intimates / Unichela
Period
2025
Role
Reverse engineering, CAD & integration
Tools
SolidWorks, 3D printing, controls

Why it was designed

The existing FLOD machine needed a more dependable way to deliver glue pellets into its screw and extrusion system.

Production trials showed that inconsistent flow and pellet blockage could interrupt feeding. The replacement also had to fit the existing machine without altering the supplier's core equipment, remain light enough for practical handling, and be straightforward to fabricate, commission and maintain.

Consistent feeding

Guide pellets toward the screw inlet with fewer stagnant areas and a smoother outlet transition.

Machine compatibility

Preserve the existing mounting envelope and interfaces instead of redesigning the FLOD machine.

Blockage control

Add a compact motor-driven internal shaft to help clear the feeding path when pellets collect near the outlet.

Practical maintenance

Provide a hinged lid, visible level window and accessible fasteners for inspection and servicing.

Reverse-engineering approach

I began with the machine that already existed, treating its available space, screw inlet and mounting points as fixed design constraints.

  • 01Observed the FLOD machine during plant visits and recorded the available envelope, mounting position and pellet-feed path.
  • 02Recreated the interface geometry in SolidWorks so the replacement could attach without modifying the supplier's machine.
  • 03Built the hopper as a configurable model, then developed matching lids and side-window variants for different sizes.
  • 04Produced a physical prototype and checked fit, access, pellet path, motor placement and assembly sequence.
  • 05Used commissioning feedback to refine wall angles, outlet geometry, mounting features and weight.
  • 06Added fillets along the sloped transition to reduce the bottleneck where pellets enter the feeding mouth.

SolidWorks configurations

One model family, sized for different machine conditions.

I used named configurations to manage the customer 80 mm design, 100 mm and 150 mm variants, and the original baseline geometry within the same hopper file. Matching lid configurations kept the enclosure coordinated as the body size changed.

Coordinated lids

80, 100 and 150 mm lid configurations.

Part by part

The assembly was developed as manufacturable components.

Each part addressed a specific function in the feed path, drive system, inspection access or machine interface.

4 x 4 mm coupling

Custom adapter connecting the DC motor output to the clearing shaft.

12 V DC motor

Compact actuator selected to drive the internal clearing mechanism.

Motor mount

Retains the motor while leaving the shaft path and service access clear.

3.5 mm clearing shaft

Bent internal element used to disturb pellets near the restricted feed region.

Hinge shaft

Stepped pin supporting repeatable lid movement and assembly alignment.

Side acrylic window

Provides a direct material-level check without opening the hopper.

Original window variant

Baseline configuration kept in the model while the interface was refined.

5 mm spacer

Interface plate controlling clearance around the outlet and drive assembly.

Drive & control integration

The clearing motor had to work with the FLOD machine without direct access to the supplier's hopper-screw control.

I used the existing 24 V extrusion-motor signal through a relay and stepped the supply down to 12 V with an adjustable buck converter. The first normally-open relay arrangement activated the clearing motor too late, because extrusion began after pellet feeding. During commissioning I identified this timing problem and changed the relay logic so the motor could operate during the period when the feed path needed to be cleared.

Non-invasive interface

The external circuit avoided changes to the supplier's internal machine controller.

Voltage conversion

A buck converter adapted the available 24 V control supply for the 12 V motor.

Commissioning feedback

Observed machine timing exposed why the first relay state could not prevent the blockage.

Control enclosure

The relay and converter were organized as a serviceable external control unit.

My contribution

I worked from plant observation and dimensional constraints through configurable CAD, prototyping, drive integration and production-readiness checks.

  • 01Reverse-engineered the existing machine envelope and hopper interface.
  • 02Designed the hopper body and individual mechanical parts in SolidWorks.
  • 03Created body, lid and acrylic configurations for several hopper sizes and design stages.
  • 04Supported 3D printing, assembly, coupling fabrication and on-machine fit checks.
  • 05Integrated the DC motor, relay and buck converter, then revised the control logic after testing.
  • 06Refined the outlet, wall transitions, fillets and mounting features from commissioning feedback.
