PCB Development · Stage 01

First ToF Robot Control PCB

The implemented two-layer board that replaced loose prototype wiring and consolidated the robot's power conversion, motion drivers, and multizone sensor interfaces.

Status
Designed, Fabricated & Tested
Dimensions
100 × 100 mm Standard Eurocard
Stack-Up
2-Layer, 1.6 mm FR-4 (1 oz Cu)
Tools
KiCad 8, ESP32, DFM Verification

Why this board was needed

The early robot used temporary point-to-point jumper connections while the hardware architecture was still changing. While useful for benchtop testing, chassis vibration, motor electrical noise, and loose connectors made it impossible to maintain a dependable mobile SLAM platform.

I designed this PCB to give every subsystem a defined physical location and keyed header. It consolidated power conversion, dual H-bridge motor control, stepper drive, the ESP32 development module, and dedicated sensor headers onto one rigid board mounted directly to the chassis deck.

“Transitioning from bench wiring to a custom PCB turned an unstable test rig into an autonomous mobile platform with repeatable electrical characteristics.”

Reliable interconnects

Dedicated headers replaced repeated manual wiring between the robot subsystems.

Functional zoning

Power, drive, logic, sensing and stepper sections were grouped by role.

Service points

Indicators and labelled test points support assembly checks and debugging.

Mechanical fit

The board outline and connectors were developed against the chassis envelope.

Layout Development

Routing and placement across both copper layers.

All surface-mount and through-hole components are placed on the front layer. The copper layers and functional zoning maps below show how signal integrity and current returns were managed.
